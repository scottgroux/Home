Embrace Empty Time

With endless options for entertainment, disable your phone and mull on the world around you. 5 minute read

20 years ago, if you wanted to gamble on sports, your options were limited. All legal sports wagering in the U.S. required you to travel in-person to a licensed sportsbook, pay in cash up-front for your stakes, and be present for the entirety of the sports event to collect your winnings. Since 2018, the physical barrier no longer exists. Online sports betting has ripped across the United States, with a majority of the $150B wagered per year placed on mobile phone based apps (1). Companies now utilize a combination of location data, usage statistics, and user preferences to send targeted notifications and incentives at times when you are most vulnerable, wherever you are. What if instead, apps that are designed to seize your time and resources, didn't have access to you all the time?

My suggestion is simple and easy. Turn off mobile data access for your phone's non-essentials. Instagram? Wait until you're home. DraftKings? Not while driving. Twitter? Ditch the scroll, embrace empty time.

There are many essentials on a phone that are worth arguing for access to no matter the time and place. Texts and calls are the bedrock of mobile phone functionality. Phones are meant to be communication tools, and apps like WhatsApp, Telegram, and Line just improve upon simple MMS and phone tools. Mobile banking has revolutionized financial systems across the entire world and banking applications provide great tools to monitor potential identity fraud and unauthorized account activity. Mapping applications have reduced time lost in transit due to inaccurate location details. Mobile phones have become an integral part of the modern lifestyle via real benefits to our lives. Removing data access for these applications would be a hindrance to their functional improvement of our day-to-day.

At the same time, many of the applications that are the most engaging and habit forming present the most negative impact on our lives. Increased use of social media applications is directly correlated with greater depressive symptoms, anxiety and suicide across all age ranges. Instead of making people feel more connected, social media works to replace in-person interactions. This in turn contributes to greater loneliness and worsens existing mental health (2). Online sports gambling frequently targets young adults, supports addictive behavior, and degrades mental health (3). Mobile games are frequently designed to take advantage of persons with addictive personality tendencies via micro-rewards and forcing game timeouts on users. The time-outs leave users unsatiated with time already spent, before providing in-game purchases to stave off game timeouts. These game design aspects lead to excessive use, which can negatively affect health and behavior, as well as brain dopamine manipulation, which can lead to withdrawal symptoms when not playing (4). Applications that fall outside of the communication/transportation/banking trifecta understand they must be designed to co-opt your time and chemically drive you to engagements, taking away the empty time in your life.

This begs the question of why not erase all these cancers from my phone and move on with my life in a more productive manner? I'm a realist and understand that no one would have read this far if the article title was "Embrace No Candy Crush". I wish to support a novel approach to lessen the issue of phone dependency (5) without asking someone to go cold turkey day 1. Wi-fi access to non-essential apps would still be an option, but wi-fi shouldn't follow you from your home, to the subway, to the office, to your third space, and back. This is merely a first step everyone can take to show yourself what is possible.

The impetus is to reduce the distractions that eat away at your time while out in the world. Outside is a huge place and it is only boring when we choose to sit hunched over a black mirror like Nosferatu. When we embrace the unstructured, unfiltered space of the outside world with non-essentials cut out, we give ourselves space to ponder and become creative.

In between high brain activity periods, we consolidate memories and develop long-term learnings that we can draw on later. We provide the brain time to dream and create goals for the future, so as to avoid feelings of hopelessness and lack of purpose. We ruminate on long-running issues that have frequently perplexed us, and allow for space to reanalyze our shortcomings and mistakes to plan for alternative approaches (6). These are all intensive activities that require practice to do effectively and necessitate space from distractions to accomplish. If we don't proactively declutter our lives and remove things designed to co-opt our brain, we don't save the time to fully utilize our mind and forget how to do so in the first place.

Embracing empty time is a muscle and the honest truth is the phones and their non-essentials have made us weak. There was a social media trend in the past couple years that centered around exposing and highlighting people "rawdogging" a flight. The idea was that spending an entire plane ride with no phone, no headphones, no book, no in-flight magazine, nothing providing contraception from the experience of soaring through the air in an aluminum tube. At the time, I saw the idea as madness. Even now, I think 14 hours of empty time across the Pacific might be too much of a good thing. At the same time, the shock and disbelief online of three hours without a screen proves how unprepared the general smartphone user is to simply experience empty time. Getting lost in your thoughts is a scary thought for some who haven't entered the labyrinth in a while.

Starting today, dip your toe into the water by reserving the time outside the invisible walls of wi-fi coverage for internal discovery via external analysis. Maybe this is the first step to enlightenment and you will end up a Buddhist monk somewhere in northern Thailand. Anything is possible. More likely, you will appreciate time without your phone pulling you out of the moment, smell the flowers, and share that beautiful moment with just yourself. There's no limit to where your mind will go from there.
